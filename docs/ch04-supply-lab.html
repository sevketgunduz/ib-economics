    02

    The law of supply

    A higher price means more quantity supplied, ceteris paribus. Firms earn a bigger profit margin on each unit, and higher prices attract new producers. Drag the price for a bubble-tea shop and watch it move along its supply curve.

    03

    From one firm to the whole market

    Market supply is the horizontal sum of every firm’s quantity supplied at each price. Hoang’s example is organic grapes: at $10, Firm 1 sells 200 kg and Firm 2 sells 300 kg, so market supply is 500 kg.

    04

    Why supply slopes upward: diminishing marginal returns HL

    In the short run the bakery has one oven (a fixed factor). Hire workers one at a time. Eventually each extra worker adds less output, so each extra loaf costs more (rising marginal cost). The bakery will only supply more loaves at a higher price.

    06

    Movement or shift?

    A change in the good’s own price causes a movement along the supply curve (an expansion or a contraction). A change in any other factor shifts the whole curve (an increase or a decrease in supply).

    07

    Related goods: joint and competitive supply

    Joint supply: goods produced together (beef and leather). Competitive supply: goods that compete for the same resources (apples and oranges grown on the same land). Raise demand in the first market and watch what happens in the second.
