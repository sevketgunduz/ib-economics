    04

    The three functions of price

    Signalling: price changes send information. Incentive: price changes motivate buyers and sellers to act. Rationing: rising prices share out scarce goods. Step through each story.

    05

    Consumer, producer and social surplus

    Consumer surplus is what buyers would have paid minus what they pay. Producer surplus is what sellers receive minus the least they would accept. Together they make social surplus, which is largest at equilibrium. Force the price away from equilibrium to see welfare lost.

    06

    Market game: 8 buyers, 8 sellers

    Each buyer has a maximum price they will pay; each seller has a minimum price they will accept. Set the market price. Trades happen only when a buyer and a seller are both happy. Find the price that makes the most trades and the largest total surplus.

    07

    Allocative efficiency: MB = MC

    The demand curve shows marginal benefit (MB) to consumers; the supply curve shows marginal cost (MC) to producers. Choose how much to produce. Society gains from each extra unit while MB > MC.
