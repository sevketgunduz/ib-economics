    02

    Calculate YED

    Income elasticity of demand (YED) = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔY, where Y is real income. Unlike PED, the sign matters: positive means a normal good, negative means an inferior good.

    03

    Engel curves

    An Engel curve shows how quantity demanded changes with real income. Income goes on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis; price is not on the diagram at all. Choose a type of good and raise or lower income.

    04

    Where does each good sit on the YED scale?

    Below 0: inferior. Between 0 and 1: normal, necessity (income inelastic). Above 1: normal, luxury (income elastic). Click a product to place it.

    YED values are illustrative, in line with typical published estimates. Real values differ between countries and income groups.

    05

    Predict the effect of an income change

    A change in income shifts the demand curve (it is the “I” in RIPEN). YED tells us which way and how far: %ΔQd = YED × %ΔY at every price.

    06

    YED and firms: the business cycle HL

    Incomes rise in booms and fall in recessions. A firm selling a luxury faces much bigger swings in demand than one selling a necessity, and sellers of inferior goods can do well in a recession.

    Illustrative income path; YED: luxury car 2.4, toothpaste 0.3, discount supermarket −0.5.

    Planning calculator: Hoang’s luxury carmaker

    07

    YED and the changing structure of the economy HL

    Food and raw materials (the primary sector) have low YED; manufactured goods (secondary) higher; services (tertiary) highest. So as income per person grows, spending, and with it output and jobs, shifts towards services.

    Illustrative model: each sector’s spending grows with income at its own YED (primary 0.3, secondary 0.9, tertiary 1.3), starting from shares of 55 / 25 / 20% at $1,000.
