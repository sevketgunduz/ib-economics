    02

    The law of demand

    When the price falls, the quantity demanded rises, and when the price rises, it falls, ceteris paribus. Demand means what people are willing and able to buy, not just what they want. Drag the price of a cinema ticket and watch buyers move along the demand curve.

    03

    Why demand slopes downward HL

    Three reasons sit behind the law of demand: diminishing marginal utility, the income effect and the substitution effect.

    A. Diminishing marginal utility: the chocolate test

    Each extra chocolate gives less satisfaction (utility), so you would only pay less for it.

    B. Income and substitution effects: the pizza budget

    You have $30 a week for lunch. A burger costs $4. Change the price of a pizza slice.

    04

    From individuals to market demand

    Market demand is the horizontal sum of every buyer’s quantity demanded at each price. Hoang’s example: at $15, male fans want 500 tickets and female fans want 400, so market demand is 900.

    06

    Movement or shift?

    A change in the good’s own price causes a movement along the demand curve: an expansion when the price falls, a contraction when it rises. Any other factor shifts the whole curve: an increase or a decrease in demand.

    07

    Related goods: substitutes and complements

    Substitutes (competitive demand) replace each other: if Pepsi gets dearer, demand for Coca-Cola rises. Complements (joint demand) are used together: if petrol gets dearer, demand for large petrol cars falls. Cut supply in market 1 and watch market 2.

    08

    Income: normal goods vs inferior goods

    For a normal good, demand rises when real income rises. For an inferior good, demand falls when income rises, because people switch to something they prefer. “Inferior” describes how buyers respond to income, not poor quality.
