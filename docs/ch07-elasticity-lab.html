    02

    Price elastic vs price inelastic

    Price elasticity of demand (PED) measures how strongly quantity demanded responds to a change in the good’s own price. Apply the same price rise to a brand of cola (many substitutes) and to peak-time train tickets (few substitutes).

    03

    Calculate PED

    PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP, where %Δ = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Ignore the minus sign when classifying. Try Hoang’s worked examples or your own numbers.

    04

    Degrees of PED

    PED ranges from 0 (perfectly inelastic) to ∞ (perfectly elastic). Pick each type to see its curve, its value and a real example.

    05

    PED along a straight-line demand curve HL

    A straight line has a constant slope but not a constant PED, because PED uses percentage changes. Move along the curve and watch PED and total revenue.

    06

    PED and total revenue

    Total revenue TR = P × Q. When the price changes, the firm gains one rectangle and loses another. Which is bigger depends on PED (Hoang Figs 7.12 and 7.13).

    The rule to remember (Hoang Table 7.3)

    PEDPrice risePrice cutAdvice to the firm
    Inelastic (< 1)TR risesTR fallsRaise price
    Unit elastic (= 1)TR unchangedTR unchangedPrice change does not affect TR
    Elastic (> 1)TR fallsTR risesCut price
    07

    What makes demand elastic? Remember TINS

    Time (more elastic in the long run) · Income share (a large share of income means more elastic) · Necessity (necessities and addictive goods are inelastic) · Substitutes (more and closer substitutes mean more elastic; this is the key determinant). Classify each product.

    Tip (Hoang p. 97): how broadly a product is defined matters. “Food” is very price inelastic, but “salmon” is elastic because shoppers can switch to other fish.

    08

    Using PED: firms and governments

    Firms use PED to set prices; governments use it to choose what to tax.

    A. Price discrimination at a cinema

    Adults and students have different PEDs. Set a price for each group, or charge everyone the same.

    B. Which goods should be taxed?

    A $2 indirect tax per unit. Compare a good with inelastic demand and one with elastic demand (Hoang Fig 7.15).

    09

    Primary commodities vs manufactured goods HL

    Primary commodities (crude oil, copper, rice, coffee) have lower PED than manufactured products. They are necessities or essential inputs with few substitutes, and they are a small share of the final product’s price. The result is volatile prices. Simulate a poor harvest or a supply cut.
