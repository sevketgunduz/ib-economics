    02

    Start with free trade

    A small country is a price taker: it can buy or sell any amount at the world price (Pw), so world supply is a horizontal line. Without trade the domestic price would be $14. Move the world price.

    Teaching model for the whole lab: domestic Qd = 120 − 5P and Qs = 5P − 20 (million units). From section 03 the world price is fixed at $8.

    03

    Tariff

    A tariff is a tax on imports. The world supply line rises to Pw + tariff; domestic output rises, consumption falls and imports shrink (Hoang Fig 28.2). The areas: a = transfer from consumers to domestic producers, b and d = welfare loss, c = tariff revenue.

    04

    Quota

    A quota limits the quantity of imports. Supply becomes domestic supply plus the quota (Sdomestic + quota), and the price rises until demand equals that supply (Figs 28.7–28.9). Who gets the gap between the domestic and world price, the quota rent, depends on the import licences.

    05

    Production subsidy

    The government pays domestic producers a subsidy per unit. Domestic supply shifts down, so domestic firms supply more at the world price. Consumers still pay Pw, but imports fall (Figs 28.10 and 28.12).

    06

    Tariff vs quota vs subsidy: same goal, different winners

    Using your settings above, compare the stakeholders. A $2 tariff, a 40m quota and a $2 subsidy all protect the same industry, but each spreads the gains and losses differently.

    StakeholderTariffQuotaSubsidy

    Consumers pay for a tariff or quota through higher prices; with a subsidy, taxpayers pay instead and the consumer price does not change. Every tool creates a welfare loss, and all of them hurt foreign producers, who may retaliate.
