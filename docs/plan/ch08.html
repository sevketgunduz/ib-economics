Elasticity of demand: income elasticity of demand (YED)

Syllabus 2.5 Elasticities of demand – income elasticity of demand (YED)Textbook pp. 104–110Sessions 2 HL · 1 SLConcepts change, economic well-being, choice

As countries and households get richer, which industries boom, which shrink, and how should firms and governments plan for this?

Session 1 common SL/HL; session 2 HL only: importance of YED for firms and in explaining changes in the sectoral structure of the economy (pp. 109–110).

Key terms and definitions

Income elasticity of demand (YED) The responsiveness of demand to a change in consumers' real income: YED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔY.
Real income Income adjusted for inflation, measuring actual purchasing power.
Income elastic demand YED > 1: demand rises by a larger percentage than income; typical of luxury goods and many services.
Income inelastic demand 0 < YED < 1: demand rises by a smaller percentage than income; typical of necessities.
Normal good A good with positive YED, whose demand rises as income rises; includes necessities and luxuries.
Necessity A normal good with income inelastic demand (YED between 0 and 1).
Luxury good A normal good with income elastic demand (YED > 1).
Inferior good A good with negative YED, whose demand falls as income rises because consumers switch to preferred alternatives; the term refers to affordability, not quality.
Significance of the sign of YED The sign must always be shown: positive YED indicates a normal good and negative YED an inferior good.
Engel curve A curve showing the relationship between real income (vertical axis) and quantity demanded (horizontal axis); upward sloping for normal goods and backward bending or downward sloping for inferior goods.
Services Intangible products (e.g. tourism, dining out, financial services) whose demand tends to be income elastic.
Business cycle HLFluctuations in economic activity and real GDP over time, alternating between booms and recessions.
Recession-proof goods HLGoods whose demand holds up or rises in a downturn, such as inferior goods and some necessities.
Sectoral structure HLThe share of output and employment in the primary (agriculture, mining), secondary (manufacturing) and tertiary (services) sectors.
Sectoral change HLThe shift of output and employment from primary to secondary and then tertiary sectors as incomes rise, partly because demand for their products has increasingly high YED.

Scenario-based explanations

Scenario 1 · Change

Tourism in Thailand

International tourist arrivals to Thailand rose from about 10 million in 2000 to nearly 40 million in 2019, driven by rising incomes in China and elsewhere in Asia. Chinese real incomes roughly quadrupled over the period. Tourism fell by over 80% in 2020 when incomes and travel collapsed.

Ask the class
• Is demand for Thai holidays income elastic or inelastic?
• Why is the tourism sector so vulnerable in recessions?
• (HL) What does this imply for Thailand's sectoral structure?
Step-by-step explanation
  1. Arrivals rose much faster than income in percentage terms, so YED > 1: holidays abroad are an income elastic service (Hoang Fig 8.10).
  2. When incomes fall, demand for income elastic goods falls proportionately more, so tourism revenue is very volatile.
  3. (HL) As source-country incomes grow, spending shifts towards services, expanding Thailand's tertiary sector.
  4. (HL) Evaluation: heavy dependence on income elastic foreign demand raises growth in booms but amplifies downturns; diversification reduces risk.
DiagramEngel curve for foreign holidays: shallow slope (income on vertical axis) showing large quantity increase for a small income increase; label YED > 1.
Scenario 2 · Economic well-being

Fast food and discount supermarkets in a recession

During the 2008–09 recession, McDonald's reported rising global sales while many full-service restaurants closed. In the UK, discount supermarkets Aldi and Lidl grew their market share from about 5% to over 10% between 2008 and 2015.

Ask the class
• What type of goods are fast food and discount groceries for many consumers?
• Show the effect of falling income.
• Why might these goods become normal goods for some consumers?
Step-by-step explanation
  1. For many households these are inferior goods with negative YED.
  2. As real income falls, demand for them rises (demand curve shifts right) while demand for restaurant dining (a luxury) falls.
  3. On an Engel curve, the inferior good section slopes backward: quantity falls as income rises.
  4. Classification depends on context: for low-income households fast food may be a normal good (Top tip, p. 108), so firms must know their customer groups.
DiagramTwo demand diagrams: fast food D1 → D2 right; restaurant meals D1 → D2 left; plus an Engel curve for an inferior good (Fig 8.7).
Scenario 3 · Change

Rice in India as incomes rise

Indian real per capita income roughly doubled between 2005 and 2019, but per capita rice consumption was roughly flat, while spending on dairy, eggs, meat and processed food grew rapidly.

Ask the class
• Estimate the YED for rice.
• Classify rice and dairy.
• What happens to farmers who grow only staples?
Step-by-step explanation
  1. Income rose by about 100% while rice quantity changed by close to 0%, so YED ≈ 0: rice is a necessity with income inelastic demand, possibly inferior for richer households.
  2. Dairy and processed food have higher positive YED, so demand grows with income.
  3. Farmers growing only staples see little growth in demand; their share of national income falls.
  4. (HL link) This illustrates sectoral change: resources move to higher-YED products and sectors.
DiagramEngel curves: steep near-vertical line for rice (income inelastic), shallower upward curve for dairy (more income elastic).
Scenario 4 · Choice

(HL) A luxury carmaker plans for the business cycle

A German luxury carmaker estimates its YED at +2.4. Forecasters predict that real incomes in its main markets will fall by 2% next year. The firm currently plans to build 400,000 cars.

Ask the class
• Estimate the change in demand.
• How should the firm respond?
• What limits the usefulness of the estimate?
Step-by-step explanation
  1. %ΔQd = YED × %ΔY = 2.4 × (−2%) = −4.8%.
  2. Demand falls by about 19,200 cars (4.8% of 400,000), so the firm should cut planned production, reduce stock and delay hiring.
  3. It might diversify into smaller, cheaper models with lower YED to stabilise revenue.
  4. Estimates are based on past data and assume ceteris paribus; changes in tastes, exchange rates or electric-car policy may matter more than income.
DiagramEngel curve for luxury cars (YED > 1) with income falling from Y1 to Y2 and quantity falling from Q1 to Q2 by a larger proportion.

Diagrams: how to draw them

Engel curve for a normal good (income elastic and income inelastic) SL/HL

Fig 8.4, p. 106; Fig 8.5 and Fig 8.6, p. 107

  1. Label the vertical axis Real income (Y) and the horizontal axis Quantity demanded (Q).
  2. Income elastic: draw a relatively flat upward-sloping curve; show a small rise in Y (e.g. +8%) giving a large rise in Q (+20%).
  3. Income inelastic: draw a relatively steep upward-sloping curve; show the same rise in Y giving a small rise in Q (+2%).
  4. Label each with its YED value.
Common errorsPutting income on the horizontal axis without saying so; confusing an Engel curve with a demand curve (price is not on the axis).

Simulation and data activities

Engel curve builder in Desmos · https://www.desmos.com/calculator

Students enter three income–quantity data sets (fine wine, rice, instant noodles) as tables in Desmos and plot them with income on the vertical axis. They add sliders to create a fitted curve and read off the percentage changes between two points. Each student calculates YED for each good and labels the graph. Pairs compare curves and explain which shape matches each YED range.

Debrief
• How does the shape of the Engel curve show the size of YED?
• Why does the inferior-good curve bend back?
• Why must we use real income?

Our World in Data – employment by sector (HL) · https://ourworldindata.org

HL students select two countries at different stages of development and chart sectoral employment shares from 1960 onwards. They add GDP per capita and identify the point at which services overtook industry. In groups they explain the pattern using YED and write one limitation of this explanation. Groups display charts side by side for a gallery walk.

Debrief
• Does rising income explain all of the change?
• What role do trade and technology play?
• Is a large service sector always a sign of development?

Household budget survey data (Engel's law)

Students look at the share of household spending on food across income deciles in one country. They chart the results in Google Sheets and observe that the share falls as income rises. They link this to income inelastic demand for food and discuss why rich households still spend more in absolute terms.

Debrief
• What does Engel's law say about food?
• How does this relate to YED between 0 and 1?

Worked calculations

Problem 1. Income rises by 10% and demand for rice rises by 5% (Hoang p. 105). Calculate and interpret YED.

SolutionYED = 5% ÷ 10% = +0.5. Positive and below 1: rice is a normal good and a necessity (income inelastic).

Problem 2. Income rises by 6% and demand for diamond rings rises by 10% (p. 106). Calculate and interpret YED.

SolutionYED = 10% ÷ 6% = +1.67. Above 1: a luxury with income elastic demand.

Problem 3. Fresh flowers have YED +2.1. Income falls by 10% (p. 106). Calculate the change in demand.

Solution%ΔQd = YED × %ΔY = 2.1 × (−10%) = −21%. Demand falls by 21%.

Problem 4. Sausages have YED −0.25 and income rises by 3% (practice 8.2, p. 108). Calculate the change in demand.

Solution%ΔQd = −0.25 × 3% = −0.75%. Demand falls by 0.75%; sausages are an inferior good.

Problem 5. Average income rises from $40,000 to $42,000 and demand for bus journeys falls from 500 to 480 per person per year. Calculate YED.

Solution%ΔY = 2,000 ÷ 40,000 × 100 = +5%. %ΔQd = −20 ÷ 500 × 100 = −4%. YED = −4 ÷ 5 = −0.8: bus travel is an inferior good here.

Problem 6. (HL) A firm with YED +2.4 plans output of 400,000 units. Income is forecast to fall by 2%. Estimate the change in quantity demanded. HL

Solution%ΔQd = 2.4 × (−2%) = −4.8%. Change = 0.048 × 400,000 = −19,200 units, so demand falls to about 380,800.

Misconceptions and textbook errata

  • Inferior goods are poor-quality goods. 'Inferior' describes the response of demand to income, not quality (p. 108).
  • The sign of YED can be ignored, as with PED. The sign is essential: it shows whether the good is normal or inferior (p. 108).
  • All normal goods are luxuries. Luxuries are normal goods with YED > 1; necessities are normal goods with YED between 0 and 1 (p. 106).
  • YED is the same as the income effect of a price change. YED measures the response to a change in income itself, not to a change in the good's price (p. 109).
  • A good is always inferior or always normal. Classification depends on the income level and the country (bicycles, public transport) (p. 108).
  • Using 'price elastic' to describe YED. Use 'income elastic' and 'income inelastic'.
Textbook errata: correct these in class
  • pp. 104–105 margin definitions and Fig 8.8 print 'PED > 1' and 'PED < 1' where YED is meant; correct to YED.
  • p. 106 says normal goods' YED 'can be price elastic or price inelastic'; it should read income elastic or income inelastic.

Checklist questions (exit tickets)

1. Write the YED formula.

YED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔY.

2. Why must you always show the sign of YED?

It shows whether the good is normal (positive) or inferior (negative).

3. YED = +0.4. Classify the good.

A normal good and a necessity (income inelastic).

4. YED = +1.8. Classify the good.

A normal good and a luxury (income elastic).

5. YED = −0.6. Classify the good.

An inferior good.

6. Income rises 5%, demand rises 15%. YED?

+3: income elastic.

7. What is on the axes of an Engel curve?

Real income (vertical) and quantity demanded (horizontal).

8. How does an Engel curve for an inferior good look?

It bends back so that quantity falls as income rises beyond a point.

9. Does 'inferior' mean poor quality?

No: it means demand falls when income rises.

10. YED −0.25, income +3%. Change in demand?

Falls by 0.75%.

11. (HL) Why do firms selling luxuries face volatile demand?

With YED > 1, demand changes proportionately more than income over the business cycle.

12. (HL) Why does the tertiary sector grow as economies develop?

Services have higher YED than primary and manufactured goods, so demand for them grows faster as incomes rise.
