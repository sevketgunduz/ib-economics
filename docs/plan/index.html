IB Economics Course Plan (SL & HL)

Aligned with the IB Diploma Programme Economics guide (first assessment 2022) and Hoang, Wray & Chakraborty, Economics for the IB Diploma (Hodder, 2020)

Introduction

A two-year teaching plan that maps every syllabus bullet in the IB guide to the Hoang textbook, breaks each chapter into 90-minute sessions, and gives you exam-ready definitions, scenario-based explanations, diagram guidance, simulation activities and short checklist questions. HL-only material is flagged throughout, so the same plan works for SL (about 150 hours) and HL (about 240 hours).

How to use this plan

  • Choose HL or SL at the top: HL-only sessions, terms, diagrams and calculations disappear in SL view.
  • Each chapter opens with a syllabus-alignment table (IB guide bullet → Hoang pages → level → assessment objective).
  • Sessions follow the same 90-minute model; adapt timings to your timetable (two 45-minute lessons = one session).
  • Scenarios are written to be projected: read the context, pose the questions, then reveal the step-by-step explanation.
  • Diagram cards list the exact labels examiners expect; links marked 'Course Simulator' open the matching interactive diagram.
  • Checklists are exit tickets: answers are hidden until clicked, and ticks are remembered in your browser.
  • Textbook errata are listed under 'Misconceptions' so you can correct them before students meet them.

The standard 90-minute session

TimePhaseWhat happens
10 minHook / scenarioOpen with a short real-world scenario, headline or data point; students predict the outcome before any theory.
20 minKey terms & direct instructionIntroduce 4–6 key terms with precise definitions; model the reasoning chain (cause → mechanism → effect).
25 minDiagram lab / simulationBuild the diagram step by step (axes, curves, equilibrium, shift, new equilibrium) or run a Course Simulator / classroom experiment.
20 minScenario applicationPairs or groups apply the model to a new scenario; one group presents, others critique the diagram and the chain of reasoning.
10 minChecklist / exit ticket3–5 checklist questions from the chapter; students self-mark and tick off in the planner.
5 minHomework setTextbook reading, a data question or an exam-style question; flag the next session's hook.

The nine key concepts

ConceptMeaning in this course
ScarcityResources are finite relative to unlimited wants, so every society must allocate them.
ChoiceScarcity forces decisions; every choice carries an opportunity cost.
EfficiencyMaking the best use of scarce resources – allocative (MB = MC) and productive (min AC).
EquityFairness in the distribution of income, wealth and opportunity – a normative judgement.
Economic well-beingLevels of prosperity, economic satisfaction and standards of living of individuals and societies.
SustainabilityMeeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
ChangeEconomies are dynamic: markets, technology, policy and preferences constantly shift.
InterdependenceHouseholds, firms, governments and countries depend on each other's decisions.
InterventionGovernment action in markets or the macroeconomy to change outcomes.

Criteria (per commentary)

CriterionWhat examiners look forMarks
A DiagramsRelevant, accurate and labelled diagrams, with a full explanation linked to the article.3
B TerminologyRelevant economic terminology used appropriately throughout.2
C Application and analysisTheory applied to the article with effective economic analysis.3
D Key conceptThe chosen key concept is identified and its link to the article developed.3
E EvaluationJudgements supported by effective, reasoned arguments.3

Criteria A–E are marked for each commentary (14 marks × 3 = 42); criterion F is marked once for the portfolio (3), giving 45 marks in total. Check the current guide for the exact descriptor wording.

IA workshop sequence (12 sessions)

SessionActivity
IA 1 · LaunchExplain the rubric; analyse two sample commentaries from the 'IA examples' folder against the criteria; students choose a Unit 2 article (≤ 1 year old).
IA 2 · Diagram & theory clinicStudents draft the diagram(s) and theory paragraphs; peer check labels and the link to article evidence.
IA 3 · Key-concept lens & evaluationStudents choose a key concept and write evaluation (stakeholders, short vs long run, assumptions, priorities).
IA 4 · Feedback cycleTeacher gives one round of written feedback (as the guide allows); students revise and finalise commentary 1.
IA 5–7 · Commentary 2 (Unit 3)Repeat the article → diagram → analysis → concept → evaluation cycle for a macroeconomics article.
IA 8–10 · Commentary 3 (Unit 4)Repeat for a global-economy article; check that all three key concepts differ.
IA 11–12 · Portfolio checkWord counts, cover sheets, article dates, source references and final submission.
